Who they are

A pure-play specialty foundry, not a leading-edge digital one. Their customers design analog, power and sensor chips and have no fab of their own.

The basics

  • Founded 1992, headquarters in Tessenderlo, Belgium. Listed on Euronext Paris as XFAB.
  • About 4,300 employees and six wafer fabs: Sarawak (Malaysia), Erfurt and Dresden (Germany), Corbeil-Essonnes (France), Lubbock (Texas).
  • Majority owner Xtrion NV at 61.4%; Sarawak Technology Holdings, owned by the Sarawak government, at 35.2%.
  • Sales offices in Japan (Yokohama, since 2006), China and Hong Kong, Korea and Taiwan, plus R&D and support in the UK.

Leadership change this year

Damien Macq became CEO on 6 February 2026, moving up from Group COO. Rudi De Winter, CEO since 2014, stayed on the board. Macq previously ran Melexis's Sense and Light business unit and held roles at ST-Ericsson and ON Semiconductor, and holds MSEE and PhD degrees from UCLouvain.

A new CEO in his first year usually wants visible wins in new markets. That works in your favour.

Don't mix them up

X-FAB is European. If your dinner is Japanese, you are most likely meeting their Yokohama team, which historically serves Japanese automotive and medical customers, or a Japanese partner. Worth confirming before you prepare gifts or seating courtesies.

Technology and capacity

Know the node list cold. It decides which Indian companies you can and cannot approach.

Processes they offer

  • CMOS: 1.0 µm, 350nm, 180nm, 130nm, 110nm. No advanced digital nodes.
  • Specialties: high-voltage CMOS, BCD-on-SOI, RF SOI, embedded non-volatile memory.
  • Wide bandgap: silicon carbide, and GaN-on-Si (XG035 depletion mode) with an open-access MPW shuttle.
  • MEMS and microsystems, including open-platform processes such as XMB10 for 3-axis inertial sensors.
  • Sensors: gas and flow, image, inertial, photodiodes, pressure, temperature. Plus silicon photonics.

Capacity worth mentioning

  • The new Malaysia cleanroom lifts that site from 30,000 to 40,000 wafer starts per month, doubling capacity for 180nm BCD-on-SOI, with the full increase expected by Q4 2026.
  • 180nm BCD-on-SOI suits smart motor drivers, smart LED drivers and battery management systems.
  • Lubbock, Texas is described by the US Commerce Department as the only high-volume SiC foundry in the United States, and signed preliminary terms for up to $50M in proposed CHIPS Act funding.

How customers prototype

  • Multi Project Wafer shuttles with published 2026 dates, and a Multi Level Mask service using four mask levels on one reticle, which is more flexible than fixed MPW dates.
  • PDKs for all major EDA platforms, analog and digital libraries, a technical hotline, ESD support and the My X-FAB customer portal.
  • They participate in EUROPRACTICE for academic and SME access, and have cited 87 designs going to production through it over five years.

Who their customers are

About 400 customers worldwide, but revenue is concentrated. Diversifying it is a strategic priority, which is your opening.

The concentration problem

Melexis, the Belgian automotive sensor company, was about 45% of X-FAB revenue in 2024, down from 55% in 2018. Historically the top three customers were around 60%. Melexis is tied to X-FAB through shared ownership and has prepaid for capacity under long-term agreements.

The five customer types

1. Fabless automotive and sensor companies

The core. Melexis is the archetype: magnetic position sensors, current sensors, motor drivers, on CMOS and SOI. Elmos is another European example. High volume, long process life, automotive qualification.

2. IDMs outsourcing part of production

Chip makers with their own fabs that send specific specialty or older-node products outside. More analog outsourcing is X-FAB's stated growth thesis.

3. Industrial and power chip designers

Motor drivers, LED drivers, battery management, power management, and increasingly SiC and GaN for EV powertrains and data-centre power. This is where 180nm BCD-on-SOI lives.

4. Medical device chip makers

Smaller but fast growing. Applications include pacemakers, echography and ultrasound, contactless temperature sensing and DNA sequencing.

5. MEMS, sensor and photonics companies

Inertial, pressure and gas sensors, photodiodes, image sensors, and silicon photonics for data centres. Open-platform MEMS processes save customers from developing one from scratch.

Plus the long tail

Universities, research institutes and small companies reaching X-FAB through EUROPRACTICE shuttles. Low revenue, but it is the funnel that feeds everything above.

How an engagement unfolds

  1. Evaluate. Customer downloads the PDK, simulates, uses the hotline.
  2. Prototype. An MPW or MLM run at a fraction of full mask-set cost.
  3. Iterate. Usually one or two more silicon revisions.
  4. Qualify. Automotive or medical adds a year or more.
  5. Produce. Volume ramps; large customers sign long-term agreements, sometimes with prepayments.

Prototyping is only around 8–10% of revenue, but every production account started there. Selling India means selling step 2 and asking them to fund the funnel.

Competition you will face in India

Tata's Dholera fab starting at 90nm and 55nm before 28nm, SCL Mohali, and the established relationships Indian companies already have with TSMC, GlobalFoundries, UMC and SkyWater. X-FAB's edge is specialty processes and automotive-grade qualification, not price per wafer.

Your core argument

Customer diversification plus funnel. India has a government-funded pipeline of small analog and sensor designs that suits their MPW model, and a set of power and automotive product companies that could become production accounts in three to five years.

Be honest about the shape

The first two years will be shuttle runs and small volumes, not Melexis-scale accounts. If their definition of first-year success is production wafers, reset that expectation before signing anything.

Japanese business dinner

The dinner builds the relationship. The deal gets done later, in the office.

Before you sit down

  • Be early. Arriving exactly on time already counts as late.
  • Exchange business cards before eating, standing, with both hands, card facing them. Receive with both hands, read it, place it on the table in front of you. Never into a pocket, never write on it.
  • Seating follows hierarchy: the seat farthest from the door is for the most senior guest. Wait to be shown your place.
  • Use surnames with "-san" (Tanaka-san). First names only if invited.

At the table

Do
Wait for the "kanpai" toast before drinking. Hold your glass slightly lower than the senior person's.
Do
Pour for others and watch their glasses. Someone will fill yours. Leaving it full is the polite way to slow down.
Do
Say "itadakimasu" before eating and "gochisousama deshita" at the end.
Do
Use the hot oshibori towel for your hands, then fold it. Slurping noodles is fine.
Don't
Stand chopsticks upright in rice or pass food chopstick to chopstick. Both echo funeral customs.
Don't
Pour your own drink, spear food with chopsticks, or pour soy sauce over rice.
Don't
Push for decisions, open a laptop, or turn dinner into a negotiation.

If you are vegetarian

Say so in advance and mention fish as well. Most Japanese broths use dashi made from bonito, so dishes that look vegetarian often are not. The phrase that works: no meat, no fish, including dashi. Offering to suggest the restaurant saves everyone awkwardness.

If you do not drink alcohol

Say it warmly and early, and order oolong tea or a soft drink. Nobody minds, but a refusal mid-toast feels awkward. Still raise your glass for the kanpai.

Reading the room

  • "That may be difficult" almost always means no.
  • A long silence is thinking, not disagreement. Let it sit rather than filling it.
  • Decisions are made by consensus afterwards, so judge the evening by trust built, not commitments made.
  • Expect a second round of drinks. That is where the real conversation happens, so join if you can.

Gifts and paying

  • A small regional gift from India, nicely wrapped, presented with both hands. Modest beats expensive.
  • The host usually pays. Offer once, then accept gracefully. If you invited them, settle the bill discreetly.
  • Send a short thank-you email the next morning, naming something specific from the conversation. In Japan this note is expected.
