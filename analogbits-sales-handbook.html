Clocking

A PLL makes the clocks for a chip from one reference clock. A chip usually has 3 to 10 PLLs.

Pinless technology (the main difference)
  • Today, each PLL and sensor needs a dedicated supply pin and an external filter.
  • A pinless block uses the core voltage of the chip.
  • It removes pins and filters. This decreases board cost.
  • The designer can put it in any position on the chip.
  • Analog Bits has a patent for this.
  • Working silicon is available in N5, N4, N3 and N2.

The decks do not show pinless products on 28 nm or 12 nm. Do not offer pinless to an account on these nodes.

On-die LDO: more data
  • An LDO is a linear regulator with a small difference between input and output voltage, for example 50 to 100 mV.
  • Benefits: better power efficiency and signal quality, fast response to load changes, voltage scaling, less board area, less noise.
  • Target uses: high-performance CPU cores (Arm) and high-performance SERDES with many lanes.
  • The example in the deck supplies 3.75 A to a core of 1.5 mm by 1.5 mm. LDO units of 50 µm width are along the sides of the core.
  • Status in April 2025: silicon in N3P. A version with 30% less area was in development for N3 and N2.

The on-die LDO is an advanced-node product. On 28 nm it is not off-the-shelf.

I/O

  • Crystal oscillator pads: low noise and low power. They connect to the external crystal.
  • Crystal-less oscillator pads: for IoT. They remove the external crystal.
  • Differential clock transmitter and receiver (clock buffers).
  • HCSL clock drivers for PCIe.
  • Programmable I/O that supports many standards.
  • Voltage-tolerant I/O buffers.
  • DDR I/O.
  • Chip-to-chip (C2C) I/O for chiplets. Core voltage signals. Good for CoWoS packages. More than 2 GHz, and more than 10 GHz in low-loss material.

SERDES (short)

  • A SERDES sends data at high speed on a small number of pins.
  • The low-cost type supports PCIe Gen2 and Gen3, SATA and eDP. It uses very low power and operates in wire-bond packages.
  • The enterprise type supports PCIe Gen4 and Gen5. Synopsys is the controller partner.

How Analog Bits sells

Off-the-shelf (OTS)

The IP has a part number and usually a test-chip report. The customer can download the front-end kit before the contract. Margin and commission are higher. Sell bundles and re-use licenses.

Semi-custom

An OTS IP with added features (pins, voltages, temperature range), or a port to a related node. It has an engineering fee (NRE). Give the customer a clear technical reason.

Full custom

A new design, a new process or a new foundry. It has a large NRE and license fee.

Kits

Front-end kit: Verilog model, timing files, LEF, datasheet, integration guide. It lets the customer start. Back-end kit: adds GDSII layout and LVS netlist. It is necessary for tape-out.

More about off-the-shelf IP
  • An OTS IP came from a current customer or a foundry-sponsored project.
  • Arthur can enable the front-end kit. A customer who downloads it before the contract designs the IP in.
  • The IP possibly needs a PDK update. Ask.
  • Always offer more OTS IPs and close a larger bundle.
  • Re-use licenses for the next chips are easy to sell.
  • The OTS portfolio increases on N5, N4, N3 and N2.
  • The fee for semi-custom work comes from the number of weeks for circuit, layout and quality work.

Chip basics in 2 minutes

  • An SoC is a full computer on one chip: CPU, accelerators, memory, interfaces.
  • The customer designs the digital logic. That is where they are different from competitors.
  • Each chip also needs clocks, clean power, sensors and pads. The customer usually buys these. This is your product.
  • These blocks are small, but if one fails, the chip fails. Thus customers want silicon proof on their node.

Where the analog blocks sit on the die
  • PLL: at the edge or a corner, away from noisy logic. Supply noise becomes jitter.
  • Oscillator pads: in the I/O ring, next to the crystal pins.
  • POR: near the supply pads.
  • PVT sensors: inside the hottest digital blocks. A large chip has many.
  • SERDES: at the die edge, above its package connections.
  • LDO: next to the block that it feeds.

Customers ask about three things when they place a block: the supply pins that it needs, its area and shape, and the metal layers that it uses.

How customers judge silicon proof
  1. Design complete, simulation only. The weakest proof.
  2. Taped out on a test chip. No silicon data yet.
  3. Silicon proven, with a characterization report.
  4. Proven in a customer chip.
  5. In volume production. The strongest proof.

Ask which level they need. If the IP is not on their node, the alternative is a port with a fee.

A startup usually makes its first silicon on an MPW shuttle. This is a shared, low-cost run. The DLI scheme gives money for it.

When the customer selects IP

  1. Architecture: they select the foundry and the node.
  2. IP selection: your window.
  3. Design and verification. Your block goes into the floorplan.
  4. Tape-out. No changes are possible after this without new masks.
  5. Bring-up: they test first silicon. Clocks and power must operate first.
  6. Production.

If IP selection is complete, your window is the next chip. Ask about the next chip.

India and the DLI scheme

DLI is the Design Linked Incentive scheme. MeitY operates it in the Semicon India Programme. C-DAC is the agency. Most of your accounts are DLI companies.

What a DLI company receives

  • Design money: payback of up to 50% of eligible cost.
  • Sales money: 4% to 6% of net sales after the chip is in products.
  • EDA tools: remote access to a national tool grid at the ChipIN Centre.
  • IP core repository: access to a set of IP cores.
  • MPW support: money for shared prototype runs at a foundry.
  • Post-silicon support: money for test and bring-up.

A company must have domestic status. Resident Indian citizens must own more than 50%.

The next phase has a target of 50 fabless companies. Its six focus areas are compute, RF, networking, power management, sensors and memory.

How to run the conversation

Do

  • Read the company website before the meeting.
  • Start with their product and their progress.
  • Repeat their answer in your words. This shows that you understand.
  • Write the technical words that they use. Learn them later.
  • Say "I will bring our engineer" when a question is too deep.
  • End with a named next step and a date.

Do not

  • Do not say "our PLL is the best". Ask for their jitter target.
  • Do not say "we have everything". Check the node first.
  • Do not guess a specification.
  • Do not explain their architecture to them. Ask about it.
  • Do not offer pinless, droop detector or LDO on 28 nm as off-the-shelf.

Objections and answers

These answers use the deck messages. Do not argue. Agree with the concern, then give one fact and one question.

"Silicon Creations is cheaper."

"I understand. Price is important. We can give a bundle price for PLL, sensor, POR and pads together. Which of these blocks do you need on this chip?"

Deck guidance: win with bundles, relations and test-chip proof.

"We use the free PLL from the foundry library."

"That is a good start for a first chip. Does it meet your jitter and spread-spectrum needs for the next chip? We can send a front-end kit so your team can compare."

"Synopsys gives us the PVT sensor with its software."

"Many teams use that. Our sensor has a long track record on 28 and 40 nm, and you do not need the software package. What accuracy do you get today?"

"We design our analog blocks in-house."

"That is a strong capability. Many teams keep their special blocks in-house and buy the standard ones, to save engineer time. Which blocks take the most time from your analog team?"

"You do not have that IP on our node."

"Correct, it is not off-the-shelf there. We can port it as semi-custom work. Tell me your tape-out date, and I will ask our engineers for the effort and the schedule."

Do not promise a date or a price in the meeting.

"We have no budget now."

"I understand. Two facts can help: we have no royalty, and your team can try the front-end kit before a contract. When is your next DLI milestone or funding step?"

"We want a royalty model, with less cost now."

"Our standard model is a license fee with no royalty. Let me take your request to our management. What volume do you plan?"

Take the request to Mahesh. Do not agree to terms in the meeting.

"Our IP selection is complete for this chip."

"Good. Then let us talk about the next chip. Which node will it use, and when do you select IP for it? Also, how was the IP support at bring-up?"

"We are a small company. Will you support us?"

"Yes. We are in Bengaluru, and our engineers support integration and bring-up. The same IP is in production with large customers."
